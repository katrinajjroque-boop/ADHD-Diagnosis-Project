# Project Summary

## Question

Can demographic characteristics and selected psychiatric diagnoses help explain variation in ADHD diagnosis status in an adult clinical sample?

## Data

The project analyzed 100 participants: 50 with an ADHD diagnosis and 50 clinical controls. Predictors included sex, age group, bipolar diagnosis, unipolar depression, anxiety, and other psychiatric diagnoses.

## Statistical Approach

Three generalized linear models for a binary outcome were compared:

1. Logistic regression
2. Probit regression
3. Complementary log-log regression

Categorical reference groups were explicitly defined before fitting the models.

## Results

All three full models had likelihood-ratio/deviance p-values above 0.05 when compared with their respective intercept-only models. The complementary log-log model had the smallest AIC, AICC, and BIC among the three reported fitted models.

Anxiety was the only predictor with a p-value below 0.05 in the reported model outputs.

An example participant profile produced a predicted probability of approximately 48.75% under the complementary log-log model.

## Interpretation

The analysis demonstrates the modeling workflow and comparison of alternative link functions rather than a clinically validated ADHD prediction system. The weak overall model-fit results emphasize the importance of diagnostics, sample size, variable selection, and cautious interpretation of predicted probabilities.
